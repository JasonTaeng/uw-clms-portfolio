## What this is
A compact concept map of introductory linguistics:
**sounds → words → sentences → meaning → use**, plus language in the brain and in society.

---

## 1) Language has multiple interacting levels

- **Phonetics**: how speech sounds are produced/perceived (physical/articulatory/acoustic).
- **Phonology**: how a language organizes sounds into a system (contrast, patterns).
- **Morphology**: how words are built from smaller meaningful parts.
- **Syntax**: how words combine into structured sentences.
- **Semantics**: literal/compositional meaning.
- **Pragmatics**: meaning in context (speaker intent, implicature, presupposition).

Habit: when something “weird” happens, ask *which level is responsible*.

---

## 2) Sounds: phonetics vs phonology

### Phonetics (physical)
- Place/manner of articulation, voicing
- Precise vocabulary for describing speech

### Phonology (system)
- **Phoneme**: a contrastive sound category in a language
- **Allophone**: context-dependent surface variants of a phoneme

**Minimal pair (idea):**
If swapping one sound changes meaning in the same environment, the two sounds are likely separate phonemes.

Common patterns:
- assimilation, deletion, epenthesis

---

## 3) Words: morphology

- **Morpheme**: smallest meaning- or function-bearing unit
- **Free vs bound** morphemes

**Inflection vs derivation**
- inflection: grammatical features (tense/number/case), usually no category change
- derivation: forms new words; can change meaning/category

Morphological typology (intuition):
isolating / agglutinative / fusional / polysynthetic

---

## 4) Sentences: syntax

Two questions:
1) what counts as well-formed?
2) what structures explain ambiguity and long-distance dependencies?

Two perspectives:
- **Constituency**: words group into phrases (NP/VP/PP…)
- **Dependency**: head–dependent relations

Heuristic tests (not perfect):
substitution, movement, coordination

Core concepts:
argument vs adjunct, agreement, recursion

---

## 5) Meaning: semantics and pragmatics

### Semantics
- compositionality
- lexical vs structural ambiguity
- entailment (A entails B if A true ⇒ B true)

### Pragmatics
- implicature (suggested meaning)
- presupposition (background assumptions)
- deixis (“here/now/I/that”)
- politeness / social meaning

Example (implicature):
“Some students passed.” often suggests “not all.”

---

## 6) Language in the brain and in society (high level)

Brain:
- errors/aphasia patterns suggest structured knowledge
- modality matters (speech vs reading/writing)

Society:
- variation by region/class/age/identity/situation
- language change is structured (not decay)
- register/style shifting is a skill

---

## 7) Cross-links (concept → typical computational task)
- phonetics/phonology → speech recognition/synthesis
- morphology → segmentation, lemmatization, subword modeling
- syntax → parsing, structured prediction
- semantics → entailment, QA, semantic parsing
- pragmatics → dialogue behavior / conversational evaluation
- variation → robustness, domain shift, bias analyses

## Common confusions (4)
- **phonetics vs phonology**: physical sound differences vs contrastive categories in a language
- **phoneme vs allophone**: meaning-changing contrast vs context-driven variants
- **semantics vs pragmatics**: literal/compositional meaning vs context-driven inference
- **word order vs syntax**: surface order vs underlying structure and constraints
